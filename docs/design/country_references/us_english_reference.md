# US English Language Arts (CCSS) — full reference (maintainer-supplied, 2026-08-21)

Status: **RECEIVED, not yet wired.** W8 precondition for US English

Aligned to the Common Core State Standards (CCSS), which most US states use
(decentralized system — a state-by-state variance disclaimer, not a single
national syllabus the way ACARA/CBSE/MOE are). Structured around four
strands every grade: Reading (Literature & Informational, plus Foundational
Skills in the early grades), Writing, Language & Grammar, Speaking &
Listening.

---

## Elementary School (Grades 1 to 5)

### Grade 1
- Reading (Foundational Skills): Decode regularly spelled one-syllable words; recognize root words and common inflectional endings (e.g., -ed, -ing); read grade-level text orally with accuracy and pace.
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Identify the main topic and retell key details; describe characters, settings, and major events in a story using key details.
- Writing: Write opinion pieces, informative/explanatory texts, and narratives; supply reasons for opinions and a sequence of events for narratives.
- Language & Grammar: Capitalize dates and names of people; use end punctuation for sentences; spell untaught words phonetically based on spelling patterns.
- Speaking & Listening: Follow agreed-upon rules for discussion (e.g., listening to others, taking turns); ask and answer questions about key details in a text read aloud.

### Grade 2
- Reading (Foundational Skills): Decode regularly spelled two-syllable words with long vowels; read words with common prefixes and suffixes; recognize and read grade-appropriate irregularly spelled words.
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Recount stories, including fables and folktales from diverse cultures; determine the central message, lesson, or moral; identify the main purpose of a text (e.g., what the author wants to answer or explain).
- Writing: Write multi-paragraph opinion, informative, and narrative pieces; introduce a topic, use facts and definitions to develop points, and provide a concluding section.
- Language & Grammar: Use collective nouns (e.g., group, herd); form and use frequently occurring irregular past-tense verbs (e.g., sat, hid, ran); use apostrophes for contractions and possessives.
- Speaking & Listening: Recount or clarify stories and information sustained over multiple sentences; tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and descriptive details.

### Grade 3
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for answers; distinguish literal from non-literal language (e.g., idioms); determine the main idea of an informational text.
- Writing: Write routine responses over extended time frames (research/reflection) and shorter time frames; write 3-to-4 paragraph essays with clear organizational structures; group related information together.
- Language & Grammar: Explain the function of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs; form and use regular and irregular plural nouns; use conventional spelling for high-frequency words.
- Speaking & Listening: Come to discussions prepared, having read or studied required material; explicit training in staying on topic and linking comments to the remarks of others.

### Grade 4
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Refer to structural elements of poems (e.g., verse, rhythm, meter) and drama (e.g., casts, stage directions) when writing or speaking about a text; compare and contrast the treatment of similar themes and topics in stories from different cultures.
- Writing: Produce clear and coherent writing in which the development and organization are appropriate to the task; use technology to interact and collaborate with others and to publish writing.
- Language & Grammar: Use relative pronouns (who, whose, which, that) and relative adverbs (where, when, why); form and use the progressive verb tenses (e.g., I was walking); order adjectives conventionally.
- Speaking & Listening: Paraphrase portions of a text read aloud or information presented in diverse media and formats, including visually, quantitatively, and orally.

### Grade 5
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Quote accurately from a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences; analyze multiple accounts of the same event or topic, noting important similarities and differences in the point of view.
- Writing: Write multi-page persuasive, narrative, and research essays; introduce a topic clearly, provide a technologically sound focus, develop the topic with facts, concrete details, and quotations, and use precise vocabulary.
- Language & Grammar: Explain the function of conjunctions, prepositions, and interjections; use perfect verb tenses (e.g., I had walked); correct inappropriate shifts in verb tense; use punctuation to separate items in a series.
- Speaking & Listening: Summarize a written text read aloud or information presented in diverse media; report on a topic or present an opinion, sequencing ideas logically and using appropriate facts.

## Middle School (Grades 6 to 8)

### Grade 6
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Determine a theme or central idea of a text and how it is conveyed through particular details; analyze how a particular sentence, chapter, scene, or stanza fits into the overall structure of a text.
- Writing: Write formal arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence; write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas, concepts, and information.
- Language & Grammar: Ensure that pronouns are in the proper case (subjective, objective, possessive); use intensive pronouns (e.g., myself, ourselves); recognize and correct vague pronouns.
- Speaking & Listening: Interpret information presented in diverse media and formats and explain how it contributes to a topic; delineate a speaker's argument, distinguishing claims that are supported by reasons from claims that are not.

### Grade 7
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Analyze how an author develops and contrasts the points of view of different characters or narrators in a text; determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text, including figurative and connotative meanings.
- Writing: Conduct short research projects to answer a question, drawing on several sources and generating additional related, focused questions for further investigation.
- Language & Grammar: Explain the function of phrases and clauses in general and their function in specific sentences; choose among simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences to signal differing relationships among ideas.
- Speaking & Listening: Present claims and findings, emphasizing salient points in a focused, coherent manner; use appropriate eye contact, adequate volume, and clear pronunciation.

### Grade 8
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Analyze how a modern work of fiction draws on themes, patterns of events, or character types from myths, traditional stories, or religious works; evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether the reasoning is sound.
- Writing: Plan, revise, edit, rewrite, or try a new approach in writing, focusing on how well the purpose and audience have been addressed; gather relevant information from multiple print and digital sources, using search terms effectively.
- Language & Grammar: Explain the function of verbals (gerunds, participles, infinitives); form and use verbs in the active and passive voice; indicate pauses or omissions using punctuation (commas, ellipses, dashes).
- Speaking & Listening: Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when indicated or appropriate; integrate multimedia components and visual displays into presentations.

## High School (Grades 9 to 12)

CCSS groups high school standards in TWO BANDS, not per grade — supplied as
9-10 and 11-12, not four separate grades. Keep this shape; do not invent a
per-grade split CCSS itself doesn't make.

### Grades 9 & 10 (Freshman & Sophomore)
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Analyze how an author's choices concerning how to structure a text, order events within it, and manipulate time (e.g., pacing, flashbacks) create such effects as mystery, tension, or surprise; analyze seminal US documents of historical and literary significance (e.g., Washington's Farewell Address, the Gettysburg Address).
- Writing: Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant, sufficient evidence; write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences or events using effective technique, well-chosen details, and well-structured event sequences.
- Language & Grammar: Apply knowledge of language to understand how language functions in different contexts, to make effective choices for meaning or style, and to comprehend more fully when reading or listening; use parallel structure.
- Speaking & Listening: Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions (one-on-one, in groups, and teacher-led) with diverse partners on grade-appropriate topics, texts, and issues, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively.

### Grades 11 & 12 (Junior & Senior)
- Reading (Literature & Informational): Analyze a case in which grasping a point of view requires distinguishing what is directly stated in a text from what is really meant (e.g., satire, sarcasm, irony, understatement); analyze multiple interpretations of a story, drama, or poem (e.g., recording or live production of a play), evaluating how each version interprets the source text.
- Writing: Write routine analytical and argumentative texts over extended time frames; integrate information into the text selectively to maintain the flow of ideas, avoiding plagiarism and over-reliance on any one source and following a standard format for citation (e.g., MLA, APA, or Chicago Manual of Style).
- Language & Grammar: Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English grammar and usage when writing or speaking; observe hyphenation conventions; understand that usage is a matter of convention, can change over time, and is sometimes contested.
- Speaking & Listening: Present information, findings, and supporting evidence, conveying a clear and distinct perspective, such that listeners can follow the line of reasoning; alternative or opposing perspectives are addressed, and the organization, development, substance, and style are appropriate to purpose, audience, and a range of formal and informal tasks.
