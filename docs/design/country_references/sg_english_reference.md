MOE's EL Syllabus (2020) frames six core skills: Listening, Reading, Viewing,
Speaking, Writing, Representing. Primary uses the STELLAR program. Years 9–10
converge on GCE O-Level/N-Level exam papers; Years 11–12 replace "English"
with General Paper (GP) or Knowledge and Inquiry (KI) — a different subject
in substance, kept under English here only because that's where the
maintainer placed it in the supplied list.

## Primary Level (Years 1 to 6)

### Year 1 (Primary 1)
- Reading & Phonics: Decode words using letter-sound relationships; read aloud with basic expression using shared big books.
- Grammar Foundations: Proper and common nouns, singular/plural nouns, pronouns (I, you, he, she, it), and basic present/past tense.
- Vocabulary: Build a bank of everyday words (family, school, animals, emotions).
- Writing & Representing: Write short, simple sentences; punctuate with capital letters and full stops.
- Speaking & Listening: Speak clearly to express needs; listen attentively to short stories and follow simple instructions.

### Year 2 (Primary 2)
- Reading Comprehension: Identify main ideas and sequence events in simple narrative texts.
- Grammar: Adjectives, prepositions of place (in, on, under), conjunctions (and, but, because), and regular past tense verbs.
- Vocabulary: Use descriptive words, synonyms, and antonyms to enrich expression.
- Writing: Draft short paragraphs; write simple sequential stories based on a series of picture prompts.
- Speaking: Participate in class discussions; deliver brief show-and-tell presentations with proper eye contact.

### Year 3 (Primary 3)
- Reading & Viewing: Transition to independent reading; identify literal meaning and make basic inferences from text and images.
- Grammar: Adverbs of manner, possessive pronouns, irregular past tense, and punctuation like commas and apostrophes.
- Writing (Composition): Introduction to continuous situational and creative writing (3-picture format); learn to structure a story with a clear beginning, climax, and ending.
- Vocabulary: Internalize idioms, phrasal verbs, and context-specific vocabulary.
- Listening & Speaking: Introduction to formal listening comprehension tests; participate in guided conversations on familiar topics.

### Year 4 (Primary 4)
- Reading Comprehension: Handle longer, more complex narrative and informational texts; locate specific evidence to justify answers.
- Grammar: Present continuous tense, past continuous tense, modal verbs (can, should, must), and connectors of time.
- Writing: Plan and write well-structured compositions; use varied sentence structures and descriptive vocabulary.
- Speaking: Learn the format of Stimulus-Based Conversation (discussing a digital or print prompt/poster during oral exams).
- Vocabulary: Differentiate between homonyms and collocations; use figurative language (similes, metaphors).

### Year 5 (Primary 5)
- Reading & Comprehension: Mastery of text analysis; answer complex inferential questions, identify literary devices, and analyze authorial intent.
- Grammar: Perfect tenses (present/past perfect), relative clauses, active vs. passive voice, and direct/indirect speech.
- Writing: Study the strict PSLE exam format:
  - Situational Writing: Draft formal/informal emails, letters, and reports based on a given context.
  - Continuous Writing: Write a creative story based on a topic and three given thematic pictures.
- Oral Communication: Advanced Stimulus-Based Conversation; give personal opinions and justify them with logical arguments.

### Year 6 (Primary 6)
- Exam Synthesis: Refine all language skills for peak performance in the PSLE English Language examination.
- Synthesis & Transformation: Master complex sentence combining (e.g., using "Despite", "Lest", "Neither... nor", "Not only... but also").
- Comprehension Cloze: Fill in blanks in a continuous text without clues, requiring deep contextual, grammatical, and vocabulary knowledge.
- Editing: Spot and correct spelling, punctuation, and grammatical errors in a running text.
- Oral & Listening Mastery: Refine tone, pace, and articulation for oral exams; listen for nuance, implicit meaning, and bias in audio tracks.

## Secondary Level (Years 7 to 10)

### Year 7 (Secondary 1)
- Reading & Critical Literacy: Read diverse text types (Text A: Visual texts like advertisements; Text B: Narrative/Literary texts; Text C: Non-narrative/Informational texts).
- Grammar & Language Use: Advanced subject-verb agreement, conditional clauses, and identifying common grammatical errors in writing.
- Writing: Transition to descriptive, reflective, and personal recount essays; write situational pieces like formal letters of complaint or proposal.
- Speaking: Deliver individual presentations; engage in structured group discussions regarding community or school issues.

### Year 8 (Secondary 2)
- Reading Comprehension: Master advanced comprehension skills, including vocabulary-in-context questions and literal/inferential tracking.
- Summary Writing: Identify main ideas from a long text and synthesize them into a concise, 80-word summary using own words.
- Writing: Introduction to expository and discursive essay structures; learn to argue a balanced point of view.
- Oral Communication: Read aloud with appropriate modulation; participate in deep oral discussions regarding social trends and media messages.

### Year 9 & 10 (Secondary 3 & 4) — supplied as ONE combined block, not split by year
- O-Level Exam Mastery, across the four national syllabus components:
  - Paper 1 (Writing): Situational Writing (articles, speeches, formal letters) and Continuous Writing (Narrative, Descriptive, Discursive, Argumentative essays).
  - Paper 2 (Comprehension): Evaluate language effects, irony, metaphors, and complex data/visual prints; perfect the 80-word summary.
  - Paper 3 (Listening): Interpret complex monologues, dialogues, and multi-speaker debates; complete graphic organizers from audio inputs.
  - Paper 4 (Oral): Planned Response (speaking on a video clip prompt) followed by a discussion with examiners on systemic/global topics.

Unlike every other year in this reference, the maintainer's source did not
split Year 9 vs. Year 10 content — recorded as supplied; splitting it into
two years is a content-authoring decision for whoever builds this, not
assumed here.

## Pre-University Level (Years 11 and 12)

Standard English is replaced by General Paper (GP) or Knowledge and Inquiry
(KI) for GCE A-Levels — advanced academic literacy / current-affairs /
critical-philosophy, not a continuation of school "English" in the K-10
sense. Flagging this now because it changes what "English Y11/Y12" even
means for SG — it is NOT analogous to AU's Literature/Essential/Mainstream
split, and mapping it onto that shape would misrepresent the subject.

### Year 11 (Pre-U 1)
- Essay Writing: Deconstruct complex global essay prompts spanning politics, technology, environment, arts, media, and philosophy.
- Argumentation: Structure highly logical, thesis-driven academic essays supported by concrete, real-world examples.
- Comprehension Analysis: Read long, intellectually dense prose texts; analyze conflicting viewpoints, logical fallacies, and philosophical perspectives.
- Vocabulary: Acquire sophisticated academic vocabulary and rhetoric registers appropriate for university-level writing.

### Year 12 (Pre-U 2)
- Summary & Application: Summarize overlapping arguments across multiple texts.
- Application Question (AQ): Master the high-weightage AQ section — evaluate the author's arguments and apply them directly to Singapore's current socio-cultural context.
- A-Level Examination Preparation: Time-management drills for writing multi-layered arguments under pressure; refining individual academic voice and stylistic flair.
