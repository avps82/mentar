Aligned to CCSS. Structured as Operations & Algebraic Thinking / Number &
Operations (Base Ten, then Fractions from Grade 3) / Measurement & Data /
Geometry through Grade 5; Ratios & Proportional Relationships / Number
System / Expressions & Equations / Geometry & Statistics for Grades 6-8;
high school reorganises into Number & Quantity / Algebra / Functions /
Statistics & Probability strands, taught through named COURSES rather than
grade-numbered content.

## Elementary School (Grades 1 to 5)

### Grade 1
- Operations & Algebraic Thinking: Represent and solve word problems involving addition and subtraction within 20; apply properties of operations (e.g., commutative and associative properties); understand the relationship between addition and subtraction (e.g., 8 + 4 = 12, so 12 - 8 = 4).
- Number & Operations in Base Ten: Count to 120, starting at any number; understand place value for tens and ones; add within 100 using models or drawings (adding two-digit and one-digit numbers).
- Measurement & Data: Order three objects by length; measure lengths indirectly by iterating length units; tell and write time in hours and half-hours using analogue and digital clocks.
- Geometry: Distinguish between defining attributes (e.g., triangles are closed and three-sided) versus non-defining attributes (e.g., colour, size); partition circles and rectangles into two and four equal shares (halves, fourths, quarters).

### Grade 2
- Operations & Algebraic Thinking: Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve one- and two-step word problems; fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies; determine whether a group of objects up to 20 has an odd or even number of members.
- Number & Operations in Base Ten: Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent amounts of hundreds, tens, and ones; count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s; read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form.
- Measurement & Data: Measure the length of an object by selecting and using appropriate tools such as rulers, yardsticks, meter sticks, and measuring tapes; estimate lengths using units of inches, feet, centimetres, and metres; solve word problems involving dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels, and pennies.
- Geometry: Recognize and draw shapes having specified attributes, such as a given number of angles or a given number of equal faces (identify triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, and cubes).

### Grade 3
- Operations & Algebraic Thinking: Represent and solve problems involving multiplication and division within 100; understand properties of multiplication and the relationship between multiplication and division; multiply and divide within 100 fluently (mastery of all single-digit products by the end of Grade 3).
- Number & Operations in Base Ten: Use place value understanding and properties of operations to perform multi-digit arithmetic; fluently add and subtract within 1000; multiply one-digit whole numbers by multiples of 10 in the range 10-90.
- Number & Operations—Fractions: Understand a fraction 1/b as the quantity formed by 1 part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts; represent fractions on a number line diagram; explain equivalence of fractions in special cases and compare fractions by reasoning about their size.
- Measurement & Data: Solve problems involving measurement and estimation of intervals of time, liquid volumes, and masses of objects; geometric measurement: understand concepts of area and relate area to multiplication and to addition; distinguish between area and perimeter.

### Grade 4
- Operations & Algebraic Thinking: Use the four operations with whole numbers to solve multi-step word problems; find all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1-100; recognize that a whole number is a multiple of each of its factors; determine whether a given whole number is prime or composite; generate and analyze number or shape patterns.
- Number & Operations in Base Ten: Generalize place value understanding for multi-digit whole numbers up to 1,000,000; fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm; multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number, and multiply two two-digit numbers; find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.
- Number & Operations—Fractions: Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to a fraction (n×a)/(n×b); add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers with like denominators; multiply a fraction by a whole number; express a fraction with denominator 10 as an equivalent fraction with denominator 100, and use this technique to add fractions; use decimal notation for fractions with denominators 10 or 100.
- Measurement & Data: Solve problems involving measurement and conversion of measurements from a larger unit to a smaller unit (e.g., km, m, cm; kg, g; lb, oz; l, ml; hr, min, sec); geometric measurement: understand concepts of angle and measure angles in whole-number degrees using a protractor.

### Grade 5
- Operations & Algebraic Thinking: Use parentheses, brackets, or braces in numerical expressions, and evaluate expressions with these symbols (order of operations); write simple expressions that record calculations with numbers, and interpret numerical expressions without evaluating them.
- Number & Operations in Base Ten: Understand the place value system for decimals; read, write, and compare decimals to thousandths; fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm; find whole-number quotients of whole numbers with up to four-digit dividends and two-digit divisors; add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals to hundredths.
- Number & Operations—Fractions: Use equivalent fractions as a strategy to add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators (including mixed numbers); solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions; apply and extend previous understandings of multiplication and division to multiply and divide fractions (including dividing a whole number by a unit fraction and vice versa).
- Measurement & Data / Geometry: Geometric measurement: understand concepts of volume and relate volume to multiplication and to addition (volume of rectangular prisms); graph points on the coordinate plane to solve real-world and mathematical problems (limited to the first quadrant); classify two-dimensional figures into categories based on their properties.

## Middle School (Grades 6 to 8)

### Grade 6
- Ratios & Proportional Relationships: Understand the concept of a ratio and use ratio language to describe a ratio relationship between two quantities; understand the concept of a unit rate a/b associated with a ratio a:b with b≠0, and use rate language in the context of a ratio relationship; solve unit rate problems including those involving pricing and speed.
- The Number System: Apply and extend previous understandings of multiplication and division to divide fractions by fractions; fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm; fluently add, subtract, multiply, and divide multi-digit decimals; understand positive and negative numbers as quantities having opposite directions or values; graph integers and rational numbers on a coordinate plane (all four quadrants).
- Expressions & Equations: Apply and extend previous understandings of arithmetic to algebraic expressions; write, read, and evaluate expressions in which letters stand for numbers; evaluate expressions at specific values of their variables, including expressions that arise from formulas used in real-world problems; solve one-variable equations and inequalities.
- Geometry & Statistics: Find the area of right triangles, other triangles, special quadrilaterals, and polygons by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles; develop understanding of statistical variability; summarize and describe data distributions using box plots, histograms, and dot plots.

### Grade 7
- Ratios & Proportional Relationships: Analyze proportional relationships and use them to solve real-world and mathematical problems; compute unit rates associated with ratios of fractions; recognize and represent proportional relationships between quantities on coordinate graphs (checking for a straight line through the origin); solve multi-step ratio and percent problems (e.g., simple interest, tax, markups, markdowns, tips, percent increase and decrease).
- The Number System: Apply and extend previous understandings of operations with fractions to add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational numbers (including negative fractions, decimals, and integers); convert a rational number to a decimal using long division; know that the decimal form of a rational number terminates or eventually repeats.
- Expressions & Equations: Use properties of operations to generate equivalent expressions; add, subtract, factor, and expand linear expressions with rational coefficients; solve multi-step real-life and mathematical problems posed with positive and negative rational numbers in any form; formulate and solve simple equations of the form px+q=r and p(x+q)=r.
- Geometry & Statistics: Solve problems involving scale drawings of geometric figures; draw, construct, and describe geometrical figures and describe the relationships between them; solve real-world problems involving angle measure, area, surface area, and volume; use random sampling to draw inferences about a population; investigate chance processes and develop, use, and evaluate probability models.

### Grade 8
- The Number System & Functions: Know that there are numbers that are not rational, and approximate them by rational numbers (irrational numbers like √2 and π); define, evaluate, and compare functions; understand that a function is a rule that assigns to each input exactly one output; interpret the equation y=mx+b as defining a linear function, whose graph is a straight line.
- Expressions & Equations: Work with radicals and integer exponents; use square root and cube root symbols to represent solutions to equations; use numbers expressed in the form of a single digit times an integer power of 10 to estimate very large or very small quantities (Scientific Notation); understand the connections between proportional relationships, lines, and linear equations; analyze and solve linear equations and pairs of simultaneous linear equations (systems of 2 equations).
- Geometry: Understand congruence and similarity using physical models, transparencies, or geometry software (rotations, reflections, translations, and dilations); understand and apply the Pythagorean Theorem (a²+b²=c²) to find unknown lengths and distances between points on a coordinate grid; solve real-world and mathematical problems involving volume of cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- Statistics & Probability: Investigate patterns of association in bivariate data; construct and interpret scatter plots for bivariate measurement data to investigate patterns of association between two quantities (linear vs. non-linear association, clustering, outliers); construct two-way frequency tables.

## High School (Grades 9 to 12)

High-school tracking is flexible in the US. This reference uses the
**Traditional Pathway** (Algebra 1 → Geometry → Algebra 2 → Pre-Calculus/
Calculus), typically Grades 9/10/11/12 respectively — NOT the Integrated
Pathway (Math 1/2/3) some schools use instead. Course names, not grade
numbers, are the organising unit; "typically Grade N" is a mapping
convenience, not a guarantee for any given school.

### Algebra 1 (typically Grade 9)
- Number & Quantity: Extend the properties of exponents to rational exponents; reason quantitatively and use units to solve problems.
- Algebra (Expressions & Equations): Interpret the structure of expressions; write expressions in equivalent forms to solve problems (factoring quadratics); perform arithmetic operations on polynomials; create equations that describe numbers or relationships; solve linear equations, inequalities, and quadratic equations in one variable; solve systems of linear equations.
- Functions: Understand the concept of a function and use function notation; interpret functions that arise in applications in terms of the context; analyze functions using different representations (graphing linear, quadratic, and basic exponential functions); build new functions from existing functions.
- Statistics & Probability: Summarize, represent, and interpret data on a single count or measurement variable; summarize, represent, and interpret data on two categorical and quantitative variables; interpret linear models (correlation coefficient, residuals, slope, and intercept).

### Geometry (typically Grade 10)
- Congruence & Similarity: Prove geometric theorems (angles, lines, triangles, parallelograms); make geometric constructions; understand similarity in terms of similarity transformations; prove theorems involving similarity; define trigonometric ratios and solve problems involving right triangles (sine, cosine, tangent).
- Circles: Understand and apply theorems about circles (tangent lines, central and inscribed angles, arc lengths, and sector areas).
- Expressing Geometric Properties with Equations: Translate between the geometric description and the equation for a conic section (derive the equation of a circle given a center and radius); use coordinates to prove simple geometric theorems algebraically.
- Geometric Measurement & Dimension: Explain volume formulas and use them to solve problems (volumes of cylinders, pyramids, cones, and spheres); apply geometric concepts in modeling situations.

### Algebra 2 (typically Grade 11)
- The Complex Number System: Perform arithmetic operations with complex numbers (a+bi); use complex numbers in polynomial identities and equations.
- Advanced Algebra: Interpret the structure of polynomial, rational, radical, and absolute value expressions; rewrite rational expressions; solve radical, absolute value, and simple rational equations in one variable; represent and solve systems of nonlinear equations graphically or algebraically.
- Advanced Functions: Graph and analyze polynomial, rational, radical, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions; express exponential models as logarithms (k=log_b(a)); prove and apply trigonometric identities (sin²θ+cos²θ=1).
- Inferences & Conclusions from Data: Understand and evaluate random processes underlying statistical experiments; make inferences and justify conclusions from sample surveys, experiments, and observational studies; use the normal distribution to estimate population percentages.

### Pre-Calculus / Calculus / AP Statistics (typically Grade 12)
- Pre-Calculus Elective: Extend trigonometry to the laws of sines and cosines on non-right triangles; explore polar coordinates and complex number vectors; graph advanced rational function asymptotes; matrices and matrix operations.
- AP Calculus AB/BC: College-level curriculum (College Board). Limits and continuity; definition of the derivative and differentiation rules (Chain Rule, implicit differentiation); applications of derivatives (optimization, related rates, curve sketching); advanced integration techniques (u-substitution, integration by parts); Riemann sums and the fundamental theorem of calculus; differential equations; (BC only) Taylor/Maclaurin series, parametric equations, and polar calculus.
- AP Statistics: College-level curriculum focused on collecting data (sampling methods, bias, experimental design); exploring data (visual distributions, bivariate regression analysis); probability modeling (random variables, binomial/geometric distributions); statistical inference (confidence intervals and hypothesis significance tests using z-tests, t-tests, and Chi-Square models).
