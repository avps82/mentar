When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Plant-only vs. both** — side-by-side comparison:
```
PLANT CELL ONLY                    BOTH PLANT AND ANIMAL CELLS
  a cell wall                        a nucleus
  a chloroplast                      mitochondria
  a large permanent vacuole          a cell membrane
  (rigid, boxy shape — makes its     (every cell needs these to
   own food, stores water)            control itself and get energy)
```

**Simple cell diagram (in words)**:
```
PLANT CELL:  [cell wall] surrounds [cell membrane, nucleus,
              mitochondria, chloroplast, large vacuole]
ANIMAL CELL: [cell membrane] surrounds [nucleus, mitochondria]
              -- no cell wall, no chloroplast, no large vacuole
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give ONE cell part and ask whether it's plant-only or found in both.
- A cell wall gives plant cells their fixed, boxy shape; animal cells have no wall so
  they're a more flexible, rounded shape — a useful visual anchor.
- Chloroplasts (for photosynthesis) and a large permanent vacuole (for water storage) are
  the other two plant-only structures at this level; don't introduce animal-only structures
  unless the node explicitly covers them.
