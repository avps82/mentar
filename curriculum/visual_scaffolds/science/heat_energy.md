When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Heat source vs. not a heat source** — side-by-side comparison:
```
GIVES OUT HEAT (a heat source)   DOES NOT GIVE OUT HEAT
  the Sun, a campfire              an ice cube, a fan, a mirror
  a stove, a heater                (may feel cool, or just moves air —
  (makes things around it warmer)   doesn't warm things up)
```

**Heat moves from hot to cold**:
```
HOT OBJECT  →  (heat flows this way)  →  COLD OBJECT
 hot soup                                  the spoon in it
 (the spoon gets warmer as heat moves into it, not the other way round)
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give ONE object and ask whether it IS a source of heat (gives out heat on its own) or is not.
- A fan feels cool because it MOVES air, it does not produce heat — a common mix-up to avoid
  restating as fact in a question (test recognition of the distinction instead, don't assert
  a fan "produces cold").
- Keep to everyday, unambiguous heat sources — avoid partial/contested cases (a working light
  bulb does get warm, but don't use it as a clean example either way).
