When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Pure substance vs. mixture** — side-by-side comparison:
```
PURE SUBSTANCE (one type of particle)   MIXTURE (more than one substance)
  oxygen gas, pure gold                   salt water, air
  distilled water                         soil, orange juice with pulp
  table salt (sodium chloride)            (the different parts are just
  (nothing else mixed in)                  MIXED, not chemically joined)
```

**Quick test**:
```
Is it made of just ONE type of particle throughout, with nothing else mixed in?
  YES  →  pure substance
  NO   →  mixture (two or more substances mixed together)
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give ONE material and ask whether it is a pure substance or a mixture.
- A compound (like table salt, sodium chloride) IS still a pure substance — "pure" means
  one type of particle throughout, not "a single element". Air, despite looking uniform, is
  a mixture of gases (mostly nitrogen and oxygen).
