When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Reversible vs. irreversible** — side-by-side comparison:
```
REVERSIBLE (can be undone)         IRREVERSIBLE (cannot be undone)
  melting chocolate (cool it,        burning paper (ash can't become
   it turns solid again)              paper again)
  freezing water (warm it,           baking a cake (can't turn the
   it turns liquid again)             cake back into raw batter)
  dissolving salt in water           rusting iron, cooking an egg
  (evaporate the water, salt is       (a NEW substance has formed —
   left behind, unchanged)            no way back to the original)
```

**Quick test**:
```
Could you get the ORIGINAL material back by simply undoing the process
(cooling, warming, evaporating)?
  YES  →  reversible (still the same substance, just changed form)
  NO   →  irreversible (a new substance has formed)
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give ONE change/process and ask whether it is reversible or irreversible.
- Melting/freezing/dissolving are always reversible — no new substance forms, only the
  FORM changes (see `states_of_matter.md`/`dissolving.md`).
- Burning, cooking and rusting are irreversible — heat or oxygen causes a genuinely new
  substance to form that cannot be turned back.
