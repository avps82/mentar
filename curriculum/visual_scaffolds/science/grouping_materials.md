When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Two columns** — sort each material by where it CAME FROM:
```
NATURAL (from living things or the earth)   MANUFACTURED (made by people)
  wood, taken from a tree                     plastic, made in a factory
  wool, cut from a sheep                      glass, melted from sand
  cotton, grown on a plant                    steel, smelted from rock
  clay, dug from the ground                   paper, pressed from wood pulp
```

**Quick test**:
```
Could you find this material already made, out in the world?
  YES  →  natural       (a tree already contains wood)
  NO   →  manufactured  (no tree grows plastic)
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give ONE material, or a short list, and ask which group it belongs to.
- The test is the material's ORIGIN, not what it is used for or what it looks like:
  a wooden chair and a plastic chair are the same object made of different materials.
- Paper and steel are the useful hard cases — both START from something natural
  (wood, rock) but need people to make them, so both are manufactured. Say so
  rather than avoiding them; that step IS the concept.
- Recycling a manufactured material (melting cans down) does not make it natural.
