When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Three-column properties table**:
```
Property       | Solid      | Liquid      | Gas
---------------|------------|-------------|------------------
Shape          | fixed      | takes shape | fills container
Volume         | fixed      | fixed       | fills container
Can flow?      | No         | Yes         | Yes
Particles      | packed     | close,      | far apart,
               | tightly    | can move    | move freely
Examples       | ice, rock  | water, milk | steam, air
```

**State-change cycle** — direction of change and energy needed:
```
          melt (heat)           evaporate (heat)
  SOLID  ──────────→  LIQUID  ──────────────→  GAS
         ←──────────           ←──────────────
         freeze (cool)          condense (cool)
```

**Particle model** (simple diagrams in words):
- Solid: "Imagine the particles as people standing shoulder to shoulder — tightly packed, barely moving."
- Liquid: "Now they can push past each other but stay close — that's a liquid."
- Gas: "Now they're running freely around a big empty room — that's a gas."

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Multiple-choice only (verifier uses curated fact tables, never LLM assertion).
- Give ONE object or scenario; ask which state it is OR what change is occurring.
- Common misconceptions: steam is water vapour (gas), NOT the visible white cloud; ice is a solid.
- Name the change of state (melt, freeze, evaporate, condense) in explanations; expect recognition, not free-recall.
