When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Vertebrate vs. invertebrate** — side-by-side comparison:
```
VERTEBRATE (has a backbone)      INVERTEBRATE (no backbone)
  a dog, a snake, a fish, a bird   a worm, a spider, a snail, a jellyfish
  (mammals, reptiles, fish,        (insects, arachnids, molluscs,
   birds and amphibians are all     and many other groups have
   vertebrate GROUPS)               no backbone at all)
```

**Quick test**:
```
Does the animal have a bony (or cartilage) spine running down its back?
  YES  →  vertebrate
  NO   →  invertebrate
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give ONE animal and ask whether it is a vertebrate or an invertebrate.
- Note: mammal/bird/fish/reptile/amphibian are all vertebrate SUB-groups (see
  `living_nonliving.md`'s animal group table) — vertebrate/invertebrate is the broader
  split above that.
- Insects, spiders, worms and jellyfish are invertebrates despite looking very different
  from each other — the shared feature is "no backbone", not appearance.
