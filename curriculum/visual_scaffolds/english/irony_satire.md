When explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Three ways a line can mean something**:
```
LITERAL   means exactly what it says
          "It rained for most of the afternoon."

IRONY     says the OPPOSITE of what is meant
          "What a perfect day for a picnic," (said in pouring rain)

SATIRE    mocks something in order to CRITICISE it
          a cartoon of the minister asleep at a fire drill
```

**Two questions that separate them**:
```
Does the wording mean its opposite?        -> IRONY
Is something being mocked to criticise it? -> SATIRE
Neither?                                   -> LITERAL
```

**Irony needs a gap** — between the words and the situation:
```
words:     "Brilliant timing."
situation: the bus has just pulled away
the gap    IS the irony
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give the line AND enough situation for the gap to be visible; irony without context is invisible.
- Satire targets something; irony inverts wording. A line can be both.
