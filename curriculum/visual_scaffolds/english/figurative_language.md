When writing or explaining this question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Side-by-side contrast — simile vs. metaphor**:
```
Simile   → uses "like" or "as"    "She ran like the wind."
Metaphor → states it IS something "She was a rocket."
```

**Signal word spotter**:
```
Contains "like":  almost always a simile     "teeth like pearls"
Contains "as":    almost always a simile     "as cold as ice"
Neither:          likely a metaphor or other "The classroom was a zoo."
```

**Two-part label** — what is compared, and what it suggests:
```
"Her voice was honey."
  Her voice   ←→   honey
 (the subject)    (the comparison)
 Suggests: smooth, sweet, pleasant
```

**Idiom check — literal vs. figurative meaning** (use for idiom-recognition questions):
```
Phrase                          Literal meaning            Figurative (idiom) meaning
"break a leg"                   injure your leg            good luck
"it's raining cats and dogs"    animals falling from sky   raining very heavily
"spill the beans"               drop actual beans          reveal a secret
```

**Personification — human actions given to a non-human thing** (use for
personification questions, distinct from simile/metaphor above):
```
NON-HUMAN THING   +   A HUMAN ACTION OR FEELING   =   PERSONIFICATION
  the wind        +   "whispered"                 =   "the wind whispered"
  the sun         +   "smiled"                     =   "the sun smiled down on us"
  the old car     +   "groaned"                     =   "the old car groaned to a start"
```

**Onomatopoeia — a word that sounds like its meaning** (use for onomatopoeia questions):
```
buzz    crash    sizzle    whoosh    pop    bang
(say the word aloud — the SOUND of the word IS what it describes)
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Give a sentence containing one figure of speech; ask to identify or classify it.
- For "write your own": give the subject + a feeling/quality to convey; child supplies the comparison.
- Year 6: simile vs. metaphor distinction. Year 7+: idiom, personification, onomatopoeia — each is
  its OWN device with its own structure above, not a variant of simile/metaphor.
- Always explain WHY the comparison/device works (what quality is being highlighted), not just
  name it.
