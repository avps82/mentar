When writing or explaining a grammar question, use ONE of these visual structures:

**Label-the-word layout** — annotate each word's role above or below it:
```
The  quick  fox   jumps  gracefully.
 ↑     ↑     ↑      ↑        ↑
art  adj.  noun   verb      adv.
```

**Sentence-slot frame** — blank slots for the target word class:
```
The _______  cat sat on the _______ mat.
   (adjective)              (adjective)
```

**Punctuation markup** — show where marks are missing or wrong:
```
the dog ran quickly but the cat sat still
^                  ^
capital?           comma before "but"?
```

**Active vs. passive voice — who does the action?** (use for voice questions):
```
ACTIVE:   [SUBJECT does the action]      "The dog chased the cat."
PASSIVE:  [SUBJECT receives the action]  "The cat was chased by the dog."
                                                              ↑
                                            the DOER moves to the end (or disappears)
```

**Formal vs. informal — same meaning, different register** (use for register questions):
```
FORMAL              INFORMAL
purchase       →    buy
commence       →    start
assistance     →    help
(formal words are often longer / Latin-based; informal words are shorter, everyday.)
```

**Clause check — can it stand alone?** (use for main/subordinate clause questions):
```
MAIN CLAUSE (a complete sentence on its own):
  "She went to the store."        ✓ makes sense alone

SUBORDINATE CLAUSE (needs a main clause attached to make sense):
  "because she was hungry"        ✗ incomplete alone
  "She went to the store because she was hungry."   ← attached, now complete
```

**Adverbial phrase vs. noun phrase** (use for phrase-type questions):
```
ADVERBIAL PHRASE (tells HOW / WHEN / WHERE, describes the VERB):
  "in the morning"    "very quickly"    "under the table"

NOUN PHRASE (names a person / place / thing, built around a NOUN):
  "the big red car"   "my best friend"   "the old oak tree"
```

**Guidelines for the question text:**
- Year 2–3: noun/verb/adjective only; label-the-word or sentence-slot frame.
- Year 4–5: adverbs, pronouns, conjunctions; introduce comma and apostrophe rules.
- Year 6+: clauses, phrases, complex sentences; full punctuation scope.
- Year 7+: active/passive voice, formal/informal register — each has its own structure above.
- Show the sentence first; then ask the targeted question. Never ask about more than one grammar rule per question.
